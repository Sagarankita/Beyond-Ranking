INTRODUCTION
Higher education institutions are increasingly evaluated on their research productivity,
research impact, innovation activity, and societal contributions. Rankings such as
SCImago, QS, and THE incorporate dozens of indicators that reflect a department’s
scientific output, citation influence, research collaborations, investment efficiency, and
social outreach. For a growing engineering institution, optimizing these indicators is
essential for improving visibility, attracting funding, strengthening faculty profiles, and
enhancing student outcomes.
Most institutions, however, do not allocate research resources optimally. Decisions
regarding faculty recruitment, research budgets, collaboration strategies, and teaching
loads are often based on local heuristics or administrative constraints rather than
data-driven optimization. Departments vary drastically in faculty strength, budget size,
citation patterns, and publication quality; therefore, a one-size-fits-all policy fails to
improve performance across all institutions.
GOAL
The goal of this project is to design a data-driven optimization framework—named
RION: Research Impact Optimization Network—that recommends optimal
configurations of departmental research variables. These variables include faculty
composition, research time allocation, research budgets, publication performance,
collaboration metrics, and innovation indicators. Using real or synthetic data from
multiple institutions, we aim to compute improved research outcomes by simultaneously
optimizing:
1. Research Productivity (Tier-1 papers per research Full-Time Equivalent)
2. Research Quality/Impact (citation-weighted output per research FTE)
3. Cost Efficiency (research budget spent per unit research impact)
Because the objectives conflict with each other, e.g., higher budget improves outcomes
but reduces cost efficiency, this becomes a multi-objective optimization (MOO)
problem. Traditional single-objective methods cannot capture such trade-offs. Hence, we
employ evolutionary algorithms, specifically NSGA-II (genetic algorithm based) and PSO
(swarm intelligence based), to generate a Pareto-optimal set of solutions. These solutions
help institutions understand the optimal trade-offs between investment and research
output.

APPROACH
The RION Optimiser is a hybrid multi-objective optimisation framework that integrates
NSGA-II with a PSO-based mutation mechanism. The approach enhances global search
(via NSGA-II) with local fine-tuning (via PSO) while respecting constraints defined on
each variable.
1. Initialisation
The algorithm begins with:
● A dataset where each row is an individual and each column is a decision variable.
● A list of objective functions to minimise.
● Constraints for each variable.
The initial population is generated by random sampling from the dataset to ensure
diversity and realistic starting solutions.
2. NSGA-II Evolution Loop
Each generation performs:
a. Parent Selection & Crossover :
Binary tournament selection chooses better individuals based on their Pareto rank.
Selected parents undergo uniform crossover (prob. 0.7), producing offspring that inherit
mixed attributes from both parents.
b. Population Merging:
Parents and offspring are combined into a pool of size 2×pop_size.
c. Fast Non-Dominated Sorting :
The combined pool is grouped into Pareto fronts using the standard NSGA-II dominance
rule, modified to prioritise lower constraint violations.
d. Crowding Distance & Next Generation Selection :
Fronts are added in order until the population limit is reached.
If the final front overflows, its individuals are sorted by crowding distance to select the
most diverse solutions.
3. PSO-Driven Mutation
Instead of classical random mutation, RION introduces targeted PSO optimisation:
● One attribute (column) is randomly chosen.
● All offspring values in that column act as particles in a PSO swarm.
● PSO updates particle positions for a fixed number of iterations.
PSO Objective Function
For each candidate solution:
Fitness = (Sum of all objective functions) + (ConstraintViolation × penalty)

This ensures:
● Better objective scores
● Feasible solutions (via penalty)
● Smooth local search around offspring values
After PSO finishes, the optimised attribute values replace the original column in the
offspring.
4. Constraint Handling
Every individual receives a normalised violation score based on deviation from min/max
bounds. During dominance comparison:
1. Lower constraint violation dominates.
2. If equal, objective values determine dominance.
This systematically pushes the population toward feasible regions.
5. Final Output
After all generations:
● The final population contains non-dominated, diverse, constraint-satisfying
solutions.
● These solutions form an approximate Pareto front optimised using both
evolutionary and swarm intelligence principles.

CONCLUSION
The RION Optimiser successfully combines NSGA-II and PSO to achieve efficient
multi-objective optimisation. NSGA-II provides global exploration and Pareto diversity,
while PSO mutation introduces precise local refinement. Together, they produce
high-quality, constraint-satisfying solutions that are more stable and well-distributed
than those obtained using either method alone. The final population represents a strong
approximation of the Pareto front, demonstrating the effectiveness of the hybrid
approach for complex, real-world optimisation problems.
